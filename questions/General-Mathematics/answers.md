### Univariate data

#### Types of data and displays

**A1.** A. categorical nominal
<!-- id: general-1-1-1 -->

Explanation: Postcodes are labels identifying areas; they have no meaningful order or arithmetic, so they are categorical nominal.

**A2.** B. numerical continuous

Explanation: Goals are counted in whole numbers, so the data are numerical discrete.

**A4.** D. categorical ordinal
<!-- id: general-1-1-4 -->

Explanation: The categories have a natural order but are not numbers, so the variable is categorical ordinal.

**A5.** A. Bar chart
<!-- id: general-1-1-5 -->

**A6.** B. positively skewed

**A9.** A. 316

**A10.** B. span several orders of magnitude and are strongly positively skewed

**A11.** C. bimodal

**A12.** D. categorical ordinal

**A13.** A. numerical discrete

**A15.** C. a small set of numerical data

**A16.** D. median
<!-- id: general-1-1-16 -->

**A17.** A. Mass of a newborn baby in kilograms

Explanation: Award the mark only if the justification refers to it being a label rather than a quantity.

**A20.** Discrete data are counts that take separate (usually whole-number) values, e.g. number of cars in a household. Continuous data are measurements that can take any value in a range, e.g. a person's height in cm.

Explanation: Any valid pair of examples is acceptable.

Explanation: Must mention positive skew or tail to the right.

**A26.** D. A categorical (nominal) variable
