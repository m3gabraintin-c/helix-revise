**Q138.** Letter to the editor: “Our library is a lifeline”
<!-- id: vce-english34-arg-p1 -->
*Multi-part question · 15 marks · Original material in the style of the VCE English and EAL exam, Section C (analysing argument)*

Background: Riverton Shire Council has proposed cutting the opening hours of the town library from six days a week to three, to fund a new car park in the main street. Margaret Lin, a retired teacher, wrote this letter to the editor of the Riverton Gazette.

Our library is a lifeline, not a luxury

Every Thursday afternoon for the past forty years, I have watched the same small miracle. Children who have nowhere else to go after school push open the doors of Riverton Library, drop their bags and find a quiet corner, a computer, or a librarian who knows their name. Last week, one of them, a Year 9 boy, proudly showed me the job application he had written there. Where will he go when the doors are locked on Thursdays?

The council tells us that cutting the library's hours will save $180 000 a year. What it does not tell us is that library visits have risen by 18 per cent since 2023, or that almost half of our families have no internet at home. For them, the library is not a luxury. It is a lifeline.

And what will we receive in exchange? A car park. Forty more spaces for cars, paid for by slamming the door on our children, our job seekers and our elderly neighbours who come for company as much as for books. I do not doubt that councillors mean well, but a town that values parking spaces over people has lost its way.

We are not powerless. The council will vote on 14 March. I urge every reader to attend the meeting, sign the petition at the library desk, and tell our councillors in no uncertain terms: keep Riverton Library open.
